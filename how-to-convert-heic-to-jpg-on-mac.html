How to Convert HEIC to JPG on Mac

Good news: your Mac already opens HEIC files — Preview and Photos handle them natively. The problem comes when you need to share those photos: many websites, email clients, and Windows PCs still choke on .heic. Here are three free ways to turn them into JPGs that work everywhere.

HEIC to JPG converter

Method 1: Convert in your browser (ZIP downloads, across platforms)

Preview can batch-convert photos too. For dozens or hundreds of HEIC photos, PicUplift's HEIC to JPG converter offers a browser workflow with ZIP downloads that you can also use on Windows or Linux. It decodes your photos locally with WebAssembly — nothing is uploaded to any server.

  1. Open the converter in Safari, Chrome, or Edge on your Mac.
  2. Drag in your .heic files — you can drop dozens at once.
  3. Keep the default 90% quality (or lower it if you want smaller files), then click convert.
  4. Download each JPG individually or grab them all as a ZIP.

No signup, no watermark, no daily limit. PicUplift sets no conversion-count cap and processes large batches one image at a time. Actual capacity still depends on your device's memory, image dimensions, and browser; split very large folders into smaller batches if needed.

For a mixed batch of JPG, PNG, and WebP images, or for WebP output, use PicUplift's image compressor and choose your output format. WebP export requires a browser that supports it; Preview has no built-in WebP export. If your folder also includes HEIC files, convert those to JPG first, then process the JPG, PNG, and WebP files together in the compressor.

Method 2: Use Preview (built in, including batch export)

Preview is already on your Mac and can export one or many HEIC photos as JPG. It is a convenient option for a few photos or a batch of images in the same source format.

  1. In Finder, select the HEIC photos you want to convert. In a folder containing only those photos, press ⌘A to select them all.
  2. Right-click the selection and choose Open With → Preview. Keep the images together in one Preview window.
  3. Click an image in Preview's thumbnail sidebar, then press ⌘A to select all the images. If the sidebar is hidden, choose View → Thumbnails first.
  4. Go to File → Export Selected Images.
  5. Choose a destination folder, click Options, set Format to JPEG, and adjust the quality.
  6. Click Choose (or Save, depending on your macOS version) to export the selected images.

For a large folder, start with batches of around 200 photos or fewer, and keep each batch in the same source format where possible. This is a practical starting point, not a fixed Preview limit: stability depends on your macOS version, image dimensions, and available memory. If Preview slows down or an export fails, try a smaller batch.

For just one photo, use File → Export and choose JPEG.

Method 3: Export from Photos (good if your library lives there)

If your photos are already in the Photos app, you can export JPG copies without touching the originals.

  1. Select the photos in Photos.
  2. Go to File → Export → Export Photos.
  3. Set Photo Kind to JPEG and pick your quality.
  4. Click Export and choose a destination folder.

This keeps your HEIC originals untouched in the library while giving you JPG copies to share. Note that Photos exports one quality setting for the whole batch — if you need different sizes per photo, use Method 1.

Which method should you pick?

  • A few HEIC photos → Method 2 (Preview, already installed, with batch export available).
  • Dozens or hundreds of HEIC photos, ZIP downloads, or a cross-platform workflow → Method 1 (browser converter; batch capacity depends on your device).
  • Mixed JPG/PNG/WebP files or WebP output → Method 1's linked image compressor; convert any HEIC files to JPG first.
  • Photos already in your library → Method 3 (Photos export).

FAQ

Will converting HEIC to JPG lose quality?

JPG is a lossy format, so a tiny amount of detail is lost in any conversion. At 90% quality the difference is invisible in normal viewing. If you need pixel-perfect copies, convert to PNG instead.

Is it safe to use an online converter for personal photos?

Many converters upload files to their servers. PicUplift processes photos in your browser and never uploads them. The HEIC decoder loads from this site only when you start your first conversion. For offline use, download the complete website folder, keep its assets folder, and open heic-to-jpg.html locally. We use Google Analytics to measure visits and interactions (such as page views, scrolling, and clicks). Images are always processed locally and are never uploaded.

Can I convert HEIC to JPG on older macOS versions too?

Yes. Preview has supported HEIC since macOS High Sierra (10.13), and the browser method works on any Mac running a current version of Safari, Chrome, or Edge.

Why does my iPhone save photos as HEIC instead of JPG?

Since iOS 11, iPhones default to HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) because it stores the same quality at roughly half the file size of JPG. The catch is compatibility: Apple devices handle it natively, but Windows, many websites, and older software don't. You can switch your iPhone to JPG under Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible.
