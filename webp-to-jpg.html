    GOOD TO KNOW

    A few clear answers.

    What happens to transparent areas?

    Transparent areas become white. JPG does not support transparency, so the tool fills a white background before encoding. Use WebP to PNG if you need to keep transparency.

    What JPG quality should I choose?

    90% is the default. For photos, 80–90 is a useful starting point; inspect the result before choosing. Lower values can reduce file size and detail. The slider is an encoder setting, not a promise of visual quality or a target size.

    Will the JPG be smaller than my WebP?

    Not always. WebP can store photos efficiently, and its JPG conversion may be larger. Compare the real output size. Lowering quality or dimensions can help, but neither guarantees a particular file size.

    Can I convert animated WebP?

    Yes, but only its first frame becomes a static JPG. The result row shows a static-output note. Animation and frame timing are not preserved.

    Are there uploads or batch limits?

    Files are processed only in your browser — nothing is uploaded. There is no signup or imposed conversion quota. Files run one at a time; device memory and ZIP format limits still apply. We use Google Analytics to measure visits and interactions, never to receive your images.

    Are the original images or camera metadata changed?

    Your original files are untouched. The JPG does not copy the original camera metadata, and color appearance may differ. Keep your originals and confirm you have permission to use the images.
