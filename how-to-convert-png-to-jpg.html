How to convert PNG to JPG in three steps

  1. Open the PNG to JPG converter.
  2. Drop your PNG files in, or choose them from your device. Set the image quality. The default is 90 percent, which keeps photos looking sharp.
  3. Start the conversion, compare the before and after sizes, then download the JPGs one by one or as a ZIP.

Your original PNGs stay untouched, so you can re-export at another quality any time. If the result is not small enough, lower the quality a little and try again. If the result looks worse than you want, go back up. Each conversion starts from the original, not from the last JPG, so you can experiment without degrading the image.

When should you convert PNG to JPG?

Convert when the image is a photo, when you need the smallest file for uploading or sending, or when the place you are sending it to only accepts JPG. Screenshots of photos, camera pictures saved as PNG, and any image with no transparency are good candidates. PNG photos usually get smaller as JPGs, and at 90 percent quality the difference is usually invisible.

Do not convert when you need transparency, when you need pixel exact data for later editing, or when the image is a logo or line drawing with sharp edges. JPG is lossy, so it throws away some detail every time it saves. For graphics with hard edges and flat colors, that loss shows up as smudges around the edges. In those cases, keep the PNG, or convert it to WebP instead with the PNG and JPG to WebP tool, which keeps transparency.

What quality should you pick?

Quality is a trade off between file size and detail. Higher quality means a larger file. For photos, start at 90 percent and check the result. If the file is still too big, try 80 percent. Most photos still look clean at 80 percent. Below that, you may start to see softness or blocky areas in smooth parts of the image, such as skies or skin.

One warning: the quality slider does not apply to PNG itself, because PNG is lossless. It only applies to the JPG output. So changing the slider and converting again from the same original is safe. What you should not do is convert a JPG to a JPG again and again, because each save discards a little more detail.

What happens to transparent parts?

They become white. JPG has no transparency channel, so the tool fills a white background before encoding. If your PNG is a logo with a transparent background and you need that transparency, do not convert it to JPG. Convert it to PNG again only if you need a different size, or use WebP, which keeps transparency. If the white background is fine, for example for a photo or a screenshot on a white page, then JPG is the right choice.

Batch conversion and resizing

You can convert many PNGs at once. Add them all, pick one quality setting, and download the results as a ZIP. The tool shows the before and after size for each file, so you can spot any file that did not shrink and try a lower quality for it.

You can also resize while you convert. The dimensions setting defaults to 100 percent, which keeps the original size. Smaller values reduce width and height. If the JPG will only ever be shown at a smaller size, resizing first makes the file smaller still, with no visible loss at its display size.

FAQ

Will my PNG lose quality as a JPG?

Yes, a little. JPG is lossy, so some detail is discarded. At 90 percent quality the difference is usually invisible on photos. Your original PNG stays untouched, and you can re-export at another quality any time. For logos and sharp graphics, the loss is more visible, so keep those as PNG or WebP.

What happens to transparent parts of a PNG?

They become white. JPG has no transparency channel, so the tool fills a white background before encoding. If you need to keep transparency, convert to WebP instead. This page only outputs JPG.

Will the JPG be smaller than my PNG?

Usually, yes. PNG photos in particular tend to shrink a lot as JPGs, because PNG saves every pixel exactly while JPG discards detail the eye barely notices. The tool shows the before and after size for each file, so you can check the saving before you download. Savings are not guaranteed for every image, especially small graphics with flat colors.

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. Files are processed only in your browser and never uploaded. There is no signup, no file size caps, and no conversion quotas. Camera metadata is not copied into the output, and your originals stay untouched. If you want a smaller file and do not need JPG specifically, the free image compressor can also shrink PNGs by converting them to WebP or resizing them.
